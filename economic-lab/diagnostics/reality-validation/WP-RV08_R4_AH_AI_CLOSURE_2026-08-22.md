## 4. R4-AH — maximum +12% ramp is not the missing repair

Relative to paired controls, `max-ramp` changes:

- unemployment by only **-2.09 to -5.96 percentage points**;
- terminal unemployment by **-0.90 to -7.66 pp**;
- GDP by **+388 to +2,290**;
- output by **+23.6 to +156.1**;
- total arrears by **-871 to +25,337**;
- linked/current-worker arrears by **+11,846 to +36,679**;
- exits by **-3 to +12**.

The intervention does improve physical staffing coverage modestly, but it does not approach physical need. Mean applied-target/physical-need rises only from about 22.7% in control to about 25.4%, while actual labor remains about 24.4% of physical need.

**H-AH1 — the collapse is mainly caused by firms failing to use the full +12% monthly hiring ramp: FALSIFIED AS A SUFFICIENT EXPLANATION.**

A stronger upward signal helps at the margin, but the underlying production/payroll wedge remains.

## 5. R4-AI — the four-month distress clock is a major propagation channel

`grace` produces a very large employment/survival effect in every seed/base case:

- unemployment falls by **16.0–23.3 pp** relative to paired controls;
- terminal unemployment falls by **28.0–46.3 pp**;
- all firms survive the 18-month horizon because the diagnostic threshold is 24 months;
- GDP rises by **5,081–6,919**;
- output rises by **286.6–381.9**.

Therefore the four-month distress/exit clock is economically material and confirms the earlier exit-propagation findings.

However, the employment preservation is financed by unresolved wage claims rather than restored operating feasibility:

- total arrears rise by **108,703–148,874**;
- linked/current-worker arrears rise by **248,648–337,466**;
- terminal linked arrears rise by **502,362–737,768**.

**H-AI1 — the four-month distress clock materially accelerates collapse: STRONGLY SUPPORTED.**

**H-AI2 — simply extending the distress window repairs the economy: FALSIFIED.**

Grace suppresses liquidation while allowing financially unsupported payroll obligations to accumulate.

## 6. Interaction — activity can be preserved while solvency gets worse

`max-ramp-grace` yields the strongest real-activity result:

- mean unemployment **8.86%**;
- terminal unemployment **12.36%**;
- mean output **1,824.7**;
- mean GDP **39,941**;
- zero exits inside the 18-month horizon.

But this regime also produces the largest mean linked/current-worker arrears, **422,095**, and terminal linked arrears average about **791,739**.

This is the key causal result of R4-AH/AI:

> **The model can preserve employment and production by preventing exit and pushing staffing upward, but it cannot finance that employment from the current operating/settlement structure.**

The problem is therefore not an employment target or exit threshold in isolation. It is the missing coherence mechanism connecting labor demand, production, revenue realization, liquidity and payroll settlement.

## 7. Full-need reference remains inadmissible

Immediate `full-need` targeting raises production and lowers unemployment relative to control, but it also:

- raises total and linked arrears in every paired case;
- produces **6–28 more exits** than control;
- reaches only about **57% actual workforce/physical need** on average despite targets at approximately full physical need, because rapid payroll distress and subsequent exit prevent stable convergence.

This reproduces the earlier Y/Z result in a shorter independently structured matrix: physically correct staffing demand by itself is not financially sustainable.

## 8. New source-level timing lead

Canonical month ordering is:

`labor -> production -> wage accrual -> payroll settlement -> household taxes/transfers -> investment -> household goods demand -> consumer goods sales -> consumption tax/government demand -> corporate tax -> exit evaluation`.

Therefore CONSUMER firms must settle current wages **before** receiving the same month's household consumer-goods revenue. This is a verified source-code ordering fact, not yet a causal verdict.

Given the R4-AH/AI result that employment can be preserved only by accumulating current-worker arrears, this creates the next dependency-safe question:

> How much of the payroll shortfall is a genuine negative operating-margin problem, and how much is a working-capital / settlement-timing problem in which revenue arrives after payroll is due?
