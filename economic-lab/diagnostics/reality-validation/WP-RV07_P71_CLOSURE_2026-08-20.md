## Interpretation

P69 showed that aggregate supplier finished-goods stock is often sufficient for aggregate domestic net need. P71 shows why shortages can nevertheless remain at the individual buyer stage.

1. A large fraction of shortage terminates because the canonical 42% procurement budget is exhausted.
2. The other major branch is `NO_SELLABLE_STOCK`: by the time a particular buyer is processed, no eligible supplier inventory remains available to that buyer in the canonical sequential transaction state.
3. `ROUND_CAP`, self-selection and other algorithmic-affordable termination are effectively absent in the central baseline results.

Therefore the residual access problem is not a generic search-depth or round-limit problem. It is primarily the interaction of buyer liquidity reservation with temporal/sequential inventory availability.

The processed-material branch is especially important. Even after broader physical normalization, 43.1% of NONCAPITAL processed-material shortage occurs through buyer-local no-stock termination. This is consistent with P69's separate evidence that same-month MATERIALS output is produced only after procurement and therefore cannot replenish processed-material supply for current-month CONSUMER/CAPITAL buyers.
