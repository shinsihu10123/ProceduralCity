# WP-RV07-P16 — Payroll Liquidity / Working-Capital Path Audit

## Purpose

P12 showed that aggressive labor expansion improves output and unemployment but becomes financially infeasible. P14 showed that even a modest blanket layoff guard raises late wage arrears and input shortage. P16 determines exactly where the firm cash path becomes unable to support payroll.

This WP is **read-only diagnosis**. It does not alter decisions, matching, procurement, production, settlement, credit, or exit.

## Stage snapshots

For every active firm-month:

1. **Post-credit / pre-labor**
   - current workforce
   - canonical desired workers
   - exact current-workforce payroll due under the settlement formula
   - cash before and after same-month loan origination
   - same-month debt-service payment and loan origination
   - capacity-bound status under the existing production plan

2. **Post-labor / pre-production-plan**
   - workforce after layoffs/hires
   - exact payroll due after labor clearing
   - payroll obligation reduction attributable to headcount change

3. **Post-procurement / pre-payroll**
   - exact firm cash
   - interfirm input spend and interfirm revenue
   - cash reconciliation

4. **After payroll settlement**
   - actual wage cash paid
   - exact unpaid payroll

## Exact classifications

- payroll unaffordable with current workforce after credit
- payroll unaffordable after canonical labor adjustment
- canonical contraction flips payroll from unaffordable to affordable
- procurement converts an otherwise-affordable payroll into a shortfall
- credit converts an otherwise-unaffordable payroll into an affordable one
- actual payroll miss

## Hypotheses

### H-P16-1
A material share of canonical labor contraction occurs in firms whose pre-labor payroll is not affordable from post-credit cash.

### H-P16-2
Canonical labor contraction materially reduces payroll obligations, but may still fail to restore affordability.

### H-P16-3
If procurement is the proximate cause of payroll misses, a material share of firms will be payroll-affordable before procurement and unaffordable after procurement.

### H-P16-4
If credit access is the dominant proximate protection, same-month loan origination should frequently flip payroll from unaffordable to affordable.
