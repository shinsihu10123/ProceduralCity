## What this falsifies or weakens

### A — VERIFIED EXISTING FACT

1. At the unit-basis comparison, RESOURCE and MATERIALS have **zero** firm-months in which the shadow per-worker contribution covers the wage over the pooled 24-month baseline window.
2. CONSUMER is also almost always below the per-worker wage boundary (about 0.8% support in control; 1.2% in the capacity diagnostic).
3. CAPITAL is qualitatively different: the unit-economics boundary is satisfied in roughly 76–80% of firm-months.
4. The diagnostic capacity increase alone does not repair this per-worker contribution boundary because it raises aggregate capacity without changing the underlying per-worker physical contribution measure used by this audit.
5. Canonical desired labor is more often **above** conservative financially supportable labor than below it in RESOURCE/MATERIALS. The broad idea that firms are mainly under-hiring relative to immediately supportable labor is therefore not supported by this shadow measure.

### B — DIAGNOSTIC LEAD

The residual employment collapse should not be reduced to a simple `desiredWorkers too defensive` story. Upstream and consumer sectors still have a deeper per-worker operating-feasibility problem, while the destruction of firms / employment relationships can remain a separate propagation channel.

### C — HYPOTHESIS

R4-B is now expected to discriminate between:

- labor-floor interventions that may have limited effect because support itself is weak; and
- solvency/exit semantics that may matter more if operating relationships are destroyed faster than viable firms can recover.

A later coherent repair will likely need an explicit unit-of-production / labor-productivity basis rather than merely increasing total firm capacity.
