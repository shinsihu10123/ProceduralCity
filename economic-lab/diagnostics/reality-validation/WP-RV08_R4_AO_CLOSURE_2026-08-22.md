The cumulative flow magnitudes reinforce the result. Under CONSUMER control, mean new current-period wage shortfall is about **3.440m**, while legacy arrears service is only about **0.149m**. Under M+C control, current shortfall is about **2.987m** versus **0.310m** of legacy service.

Therefore the elevated arrears stock is not primarily a historical residue left over after current payroll has become healthy. New arrears are being created much faster than old claims can be serviced.

## Main result 2 — cross-employer legacy-claim payment exists

The provenance reconstruction confirms that some current employers pay cash against wage claims that originated under another employer.

Across four-seed means:

- CONSUMER control: **42.75% of legacy-arrears cash paid** is attributable to other-employer claims, equal to about **2.72% of all wage cash paid**.
- M+C control: **47.81% of legacy-arrears cash paid** is cross-employer, equal to about **4.26% of all wage cash paid**.
- Across staffing regimes, cross-employer legacy payments are roughly **39–53% of legacy-arrears payments**, but only about **2.4–5.1% of total wage cash paid**.
- Roughly **5.6–11.1% of wage-payment observations** carry some claim attributable to a different employer under the conservative provenance reconstruction.

This is consistent with the canonical payroll implementation: a household carries one scalar `wageArrears` balance, while the current employer pays `current wage + up to 0.5 × prior household arrears`. The arrears scalar does not encode employer of origin.
