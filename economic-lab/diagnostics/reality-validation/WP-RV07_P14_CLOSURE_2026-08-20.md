## Interpretation

### A — VERIFIED EXISTING FACT

1. The canonical labor rule can reduce desired workers while the firm is capacity-bound under its unconstrained production plan.
2. Preventing all such layoffs reduces the number of layoffs substantially.
3. The blanket guard raises payroll arrears and input shortage and lowers consumer output in the late window.

### B — DIAGNOSTIC LEAD

`capacity-bound under the unconstrained plan` is not a sufficient condition for productive labor retention. The firm may still be unable to employ preserved labor productively because input availability and/or working-capital liquidity binds before payroll settlement.

### C — HYPOTHESIS

The remaining failure is increasingly consistent with a coupled constraint:

`labor retention / production capacity` × `input availability` × `payroll affordability / working capital`.

The P14 result specifically weakens the hypothesis that labor contraction can be repaired by a generic no-layoff rule.

### D — PROPOSED NEXT DIAGNOSTICS

Two dependency-safe follow-ups are admitted in parallel:

   - Apply the same no-expansion guard only to `RESOURCE` firms, which have no upstream input requirement.
   - Purpose: isolate whether upstream resource labor retention improves the supply chain without reproducing the downstream payroll/input conflict.

   - Purpose: determine whether canonical labor contraction is primarily a liquidity response and whether procurement converts otherwise-affordable payroll into arrears.
