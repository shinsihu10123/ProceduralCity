## A — VERIFIED EXISTING FACT: detached wage claims are real in the model

All inspected V shards pass the hard diagnostic gates, including the explicit `orphanClaimsObserved` gate.

Source semantics and execution evidence agree:

- a laid-off household retains its existing `wageArrears`;
- the employment/employer link is removed;
- the normal payroll routine does not revisit households without a live employer link;
- therefore valid former-worker wage claims can persist after separation.

This is a genuine missing post-employment liability-settlement state. It must eventually be represented explicitly; claims must not simply disappear.

## A — VERIFIED EXISTING FACT: detached claims do NOT explain the restructuring arrears penalty

The key result is the opposite of the strongest detached-claim hypothesis.

In every inspected seed, operating/multi restructuring raises **total** wage arrears while **reducing** both unemployed-former-worker arrears and the orphaned share relative to control.

Original C reproduces the same pattern. For example, materials-consumer control has total arrears about `108,070` and orphan share `57.4%`, while operating restructuring has total arrears about `156,481` but orphan share only `19.9%`; multi has total arrears about `171,352` but orphan share only `13.3%`.

Therefore the extra arrears generated by permissive restructuring are predominantly **not** detached legacy claims. They are arrears associated with the larger population of workers kept inside surviving firms.

## A — VERIFIED EXISTING FACT: repeated restructuring is material

Operating/multi rules repeatedly revisit many firms.

Examples:

- Original B consumer operating: `281` restructure events, `89` firms restructured more than once, maximum repeat `5`.
- Original C consumer operating: `272` events, `84` recurrent firms, maximum repeat `5`.
- Held-out E consumer operating: `290` events, `95` recurrent firms, maximum repeat `5`.
- Materials-consumer operating shows the same order of magnitude.

This means the current state machine often resets distress and retains a supportable-looking workforce, but the firm subsequently returns to distress and is restructured again.
