# Economic Ecosystem Structural Realism Audit Register

Date opened: 2026-08-22
Project: 경제 동아리 「가온」 — AI 기반 자율 세계 시뮬레이션 본판 / Economic Lab
Status: **ACTIVE DIAGNOSTIC REGISTER**

## Purpose

The economy is treated as a coupled ecosystem, not as a collection of independently tunable equations. A subsystem can be locally plausible yet destabilize the whole system if its ontology, stocks, flows, contracts, relationships, adaptation speed or historical state are inconsistent with neighboring subsystems.

This register prevents the reality-validation program from overfitting to the currently visible collapse channel. It records structural questions that must be audited before any claim that the Economic Lab represents a self-sustaining mature economy.

Claim classes:
- **A — VERIFIED EXISTING FACT**
- **B — DIAGNOSTIC LEAD**
- **C — HYPOTHESIS**
- **D — PROPOSED CHANGE**

No item in B/C authorizes a canonical repair.

## Audit lanes

### E1. Population / person / household ontology

Current status: **A/B — structural incompleteness verified; causal magnitude under study**

Questions:
- Is a `household` one person or a multi-person household?
- Can one household contain multiple workers, children, retirees and dependents?
- Are age, birth, death, aging, retirement, education and labor-force participation represented?
- Is unemployment defined over the labor force rather than all household agents?
- Are benefits conditioned on actual eligibility rather than `!employed`?
- Can population grow, shrink and migrate?

Known evidence:
- current household-agent population is fixed over observed runs;
- age/lifecycle fields are absent;
- all non-employed household agents can enter the unemployment pool / transfer logic;
- current one-agent/one-employer structure cannot represent multi-earner households.

Register link: `DEMOGRAPHY_LABOR_STRUCTURAL_REALISM_REGISTER_2026-08-22.md`

### E2. Firm ontology and lifecycle

Status: **B/C**

Questions:
- Do firms have age, founders/owners, retained organizational knowledge and persistent supplier/customer relationships?
- Are entry, growth, maturity, restructuring, merger, acquisition, insolvency and liquidation distinct states?
- Does a failed firm transfer productive assets, contracts, workers and claims to successors, or simply disappear?
- Are entrants capitalized and financed through plausible channels?
- Does firm size distribution emerge endogenously rather than remain narrowly seeded?

Known leads:
- exit cascades strongly amplify unemployment;
- replacement entrants are weak;
- simple restructure variants preserve employment but accumulate arrears.

### E3. Productive capital / depreciation / vintages

Status: **B — major realism gap**

Questions:
- Does physical capital depreciate, break, become obsolete or require maintenance?
- Are capital vintages heterogeneous in productivity and compatibility?
- Does investment require installation time?
- Can capital be sold, scrapped, inherited after insolvency or reallocated?

Known evidence:
- R4-AY observed many capital increases and **zero capital decreases** over the census window.
- This likely biases productive capacity upward, so it is not a simple explanation for collapse, but it prevents mature capital dynamics.

### E4. Production technology / unit economics

Status: **A — multiple structural defects verified**

Questions:
- Are sectoral prices, wages, input coefficients and productivity jointly feasible?
- Is production labor demand derived from economically meaningful planned output?
- Are physical capacity, working capital and profitable capacity distinct?

Known evidence:
- RESOURCE net value product of labor is structurally below wage in tested states;
- MATERIALS is often infeasible;
- CAPITAL is mostly plan-viable but suffers realization/absorption losses;
- CONSUMER is near break-even but strongly under-executes planned production because staffing is far below physical requirement.

### E5. Labor market / careers / occupational mobility

Status: **A/B**

Questions:
- Are occupations, skills, training, experience and career paths represented?
- Can workers move job-to-job without unemployment?
- Are wages bargained or posted mechanically?
- Do firms recruit for production needs and financeable payroll simultaneously?
- Are layoffs, quits, retirement, firing and temporary leave distinct?

Known evidence:
- desired-worker target formation, not matching, dominates the CONSUMER workforce deficit;
- direct job-to-job transitions are rare relative to employment exits;
- staffing catch-up at the maximum allowed ramp is much slower than distress/exit.

### E6. Payroll / employment contracts / wage claims

Status: **A — structural defects verified**

Questions:
- Does each wage claim retain the liable employer and employment-period provenance?
- Are arrears senior claims in insolvency?
- Can wage contracts be renegotiated rather than only paid/unpaid?
- Are payroll timing and revenue timing financeable through working-capital institutions?

Known evidence:
- prior wage arrears are stored on the household as a scalar and can be paid by the current employer;
- cross-employer legacy-claim payment occurs but is not the dominant collapse root;
- current wage generation itself remains heavily underfunded.

### E7. Supply-chain network / contracts / inventories

Status: **A/B**

Questions:
- Are supplier relationships persistent contracts or sampled spot interactions?
- Are delivery lags, order books, safety stocks, shortages and substitutions represented?
- Can buyers diversify suppliers or integrate vertically?
- Are inventories inherited consistently with production history?

Known evidence:
- month 0 input inventories are zero for input-using firms;
- supplier relations are built after simulation start;
- B2B pair repetition increases over time;
- opening input buffers reduce startup shortage but do not stabilize the economy.

### E8. Final-goods demand / household consumption

Status: **A/B**

Questions:
- Is household consumption tied to household composition, needs, durable/non-durable goods and wealth?
- Are consumption baskets differentiated by income and demographic structure?
- Is unmet demand distinguishable from voluntary saving?

Known evidence:
- household voluntary spending cuts are not the primary initial shortage mechanism;
- CONSUMER sell-through is relatively high; the primary loss occurs before finished-goods absorption, at production execution.

### E9. Banking system / credit topology

Status: **A/B**

Questions:
- Is one commercial bank per country sufficient for the intended realism level?
- Are relationship lending, collateral, maturity transformation, interbank markets, provisioning and bank failure represented?
- Is credit underwriting aware of input needs, production plans and payroll timing?
- Can working-capital finance distinguish temporary timing gaps from persistent insolvency?

Known evidence:
- all private loans start at zero despite mature capital and public debt;
- bridge-credit experiments only marginally reduce arrears and often transform wage stress into debt/default;
- most payroll-shortfall firms show persistent, not one-month, financial weakness.

### E10. Money / settlement / central banking

Status: **B/C**

Questions:
- How are deposits, reserves, bank money and central-bank money related?
- Are liquidity facilities, reserve management, lender-of-last-resort functions and bank settlement represented?
- Does monetary policy transmission operate through multiple realistic channels and lags?

Known lead:
- central banks exist and set policy, but R4-AY observed no active facility/operation records during the census window.
- absence of those records alone is not proof that monetary policy is inactive.

### E11. Fiscal system / automatic stabilizers

Status: **A/B**

Questions:
- Are taxes and transfers based on realistic tax bases and eligibility?
- Are children, retirees and nonparticipants separated from unemployed workers?
- Does public debt have a coherent inherited history?
- Are government spending and investment capacity-constrained?

Known evidence:
- inherited public debt exists at month 0;
- non-employed household agents can be treated as unemployment-transfer recipients;
- demographic ontology therefore directly contaminates fiscal realism.

### E12. Ownership / equity / wealth distribution

Status: **A/B**

Questions:
- Who owns firms at month 0?
- Are founders, private owners, institutional investors and household portfolios represented?
- Do dividends, retained earnings, dilution and bankruptcy losses affect owners?
- Can wealth concentration emerge and feed back to consumption/investment?

Known evidence:
- firms have equity-market objects at opening while public ownership and household portfolios begin at zero;
- even after three simulated years, observed household portfolio ownership remains very shallow in the current diagnostic state.

### E13. International trade / FX / external finance

Status: **B**

Questions:
- Are trade relationships, shipping/logistics, trade credit and contracts inherited or created from zero?
- Are exchange rates grounded in stocks/flows with plausible lags?
- Can external finance amplify or stabilize domestic stress?
- Do countries specialize endogenously?

Known evidence:
- FX/tariff/international institutions exist at month 0 while trade and foreign-funding histories begin from zero;
- trade relationships form rapidly after start.

### E14. Information / expectations / cognition

Status: **B/C**

Questions:
- Do agents begin with heterogeneous life histories, models and information sets?
- Are common public signals distinguished from private information?
- Are learning rates, planning horizons and strategy spaces sufficiently heterogeneous?
- Can agents form institutions/contracts rather than only choose from predefined action menus?

Known evidence:
- cognitive episode history starts at zero;
- R4-AZ/BA/BB interim evidence shows high modal-action concentration among CONSUMER firms and relatively long firm action persistence.

### E15. Behavioral synchronization / herding

Status: **B — active R4-AZ**

Questions:
- Is similarity of behavior an appropriate response to common fundamentals or excessive algorithmic homogeneity?
- Does synchronized defense create self-fulfilling demand collapse?
- Do private histories/information break synchronized action without arbitrary noise?

Current interim evidence:
- CONSUMER modal action share roughly ~80% in completed shards;
- household actions are less concentrated and much less persistent.

### E16. Institutional plurality and endogenous institution formation

Status: **C**

Questions:
- Are banks, firms, unions, regulators, exchanges, courts and contractual standards exogenous singletons or endogenous institutions?
- Can institutions fail, merge, split and learn?
- Are institutional capacities state variables with histories?

### E17. Legal / contract / insolvency architecture

Status: **B/C**

Questions:
- Are property rights, secured/unsecured claims and creditor priorities represented?
- Does bankruptcy preserve viable capital and contracts?
- Are wage arrears, taxes, banks and suppliers ordered coherently in insolvency?
- Is restructuring distinct from liquidation?

Known evidence:
- simple diagnostic restructure rules improve employment but can generate extreme arrears; a real insolvency architecture is not equivalent to keeping firms alive.

### E18. Entry / entrepreneurship / innovation

Status: **B/C**

Questions:
- Why and how are new firms created?
- Do entrepreneurs perceive profit opportunities and obtain startup finance?
- Can business models, products and technologies evolve rather than merely replace failed firms?

Known lead:
- replacement entrants have been too weak to offset exit cascades.

### E19. Technology / productivity evolution

Status: **C**

Questions:
- Is productivity a fixed draw, capital effect or endogenous learning/innovation process?
- Are sectoral productivity differences historically plausible?
- Can firms learn-by-doing, imitate, innovate and fail technologically?

### E20. Geography / transport / spatial economy

Status: **C — largely outside current reduced Economic Lab**

Questions:
- Are labor, goods and capital markets spatially frictionless or geographically constrained?
- Do transport costs and infrastructure shape supplier networks, city growth and trade?
- Does migration respond to regional opportunities?

This lane must eventually reconnect Economic Lab findings to the full-world architecture without contaminating the reduced diagnostic model prematurely.

### E21. Environment / resources / energy

Status: **C**

Questions:
- Are resource stocks renewable/depletable?
- Does extraction require location, capital, energy and environmental costs?
- Can weather, disasters and ecological constraints change production possibilities?

### E22. Historical-state coherence / bootstrap

Status: **A/B**

Questions:
- Do opening stocks and relationships correspond to one internally possible prior history?
- Can a mature economy be initialized from a replayed/generated prehistory rather than independent random draws?
- Are opening income, debt, inventories and ownership mutually reconciled?

Known evidence:
- current opening mixes mature stocks with newborn relationship/history layers;
- R4-AX falsified the claim that simple input buffers plus six-month exit grace are sufficient.

### E23. Cross-subsystem timescale compatibility

Status: **A/B — active R4-BB**

Questions:
- Are hiring, investment, debt service, learning, contract formation, unemployment, restructuring and policy horizons compatible?
- Can a viable agent adapt before canonical failure clocks terminate it?

Current interim evidence:
- plan-viable CONSUMER staffing catch-up at +12%/month requires ~22–24 months;
- 90%+ of such observations require >4 months;
- first distress to exit is only ~3 months after distress begins;
- unemployment and loan-resolution horizons are much longer.

### E24. Stabilizing vs destabilizing feedback inventory

Status: **B/C**

Required audit:
- explicitly map every positive and negative feedback loop;
- identify which loops dominate at different horizons;
- verify that stabilizers actually operate before destructive loops cross irreversible thresholds.

Known destabilizing loop:
`payroll/production stress -> distress -> exit -> unemployment -> demand loss -> downstream/upstream revenue loss -> more payroll stress`.

Potential stabilizers requiring audit:
- price adjustment;
- wage adjustment;
- job reallocation;
- inventories;
- credit;
- automatic fiscal transfers;
- monetary policy;
- firm entry;
- restructuring;
- international trade;
- ownership/equity recapitalization.

### E25. Empirical realism / calibration / validation separation

Status: **NOT YET AUTHORIZED FOR TUNING**

Rules:
- structural causal defects must be diagnosed before calibration;
- calibration and held-out validation must use separate targets;
- authoritative evidence should come from SNA, IMF, OECD, BIS, World Bank, central banks, national statistics and peer-reviewed literature;
- no empirical target may be used to conceal a structural inconsistency.

## Current global causal graph

A working synthesis, not a final model:

`historically incoherent opening + sectoral unit-economics defects + production-blind labor target`
→ `understaffed production / weak realized contribution`
→ `payroll shortfall and working-capital stress`
→ `distress accumulation`
→ `rapid exit relative to adjustment clocks`
→ `unemployment + lost supplier/customer/credit relationships`
→ `consumption / intermediate / capital demand contraction`
→ `upstream revenue loss`
→ `additional arrears, credit stress and exits`
→ `common macro signals and increasingly synchronized defensive firm behavior`
→ `collapse attractor`.

This graph deliberately contains both verified links and unresolved hypotheses. Each future WP must label which edges it verifies, falsifies or leaves open.

## Execution policy from this point

The research program should proceed in **wide dependency-safe batches**:

1. structural source-contract audits;
2. non-invasive runtime census;
3. causal ablations only after a lead is measurable;
4. multiseed + held-out replication;
5. long-horizon validation;
6. only then architecture repair candidates;
7. empirical calibration after structural coherence.

The target is not merely “a simulation that does not crash.” The target is a world in which economic institutions, people, firms, stocks, flows and adaptive clocks can coexist for long horizons without hidden accounting violations, arbitrary life-support rules or a universal collapse attractor.
