## Intervention incidence

### Financial-support labor floor

The P51-derived support floor activated repeatedly and retained workers without ever requiring desired employment above the current workforce.

Baseline three-seed totals:

- CONSUMER support-floor-only: `132` floor applications / `132` worker-slots retained.
- MATERIALS+CONSUMER support-floor-only: `122` floor applications / `122` worker-slots retained.

The labor floor therefore has a genuine causal effect. On MATERIALS+CONSUMER it lowers FULL unemployment by about `1.46 pp` and raises fulfillment by about `2.0 pp`. However, it also raises mean wage arrears by about `2354`. On the CONSUMER base it lowers unemployment by only about `0.48 pp`, raises exits by `4`, raises arrears by about `3949`, and lowers consumer output.

### Current-cash-flow viable exit guard

The bounded exit guard almost never finds an exit candidate that is both:

1. liquidity-driven rather than severe-credit-driven; and
2. currently able to cover `wage × workers` from realized `max(0, revenue - inputSpend)`.

Baseline viable-exit-only runs:

- CONSUMER: `227` terminal exit candidates, only `3` guarded (`~1.3%`).
- MATERIALS+CONSUMER: `234` terminal exit candidates, only `7` guarded (`~3.0%`).

Consequently the macro effect is negligible. CONSUMER unemployment improves only about `0.12 pp`; MATERIALS+CONSUMER is essentially unchanged/slightly worse.

This sharply contrasts with P73/P74's blanket no-exit benchmark, which reduces FULL unemployment by roughly `11 pp` on both bases.

## Interpretation

P73 proved that firm exit mechanically creates a very large amount of late unemployment. P74 now separates that mechanical importance from the question of whether canonical exits are broadly **premature exits of otherwise currently self-financing firms**.

They are not, under this strict current-cash-flow criterion.

Most terminal candidates reaching the exit threshold are already unable to cover current payroll from realized operating contribution, are in severe credit stress, or both. Simply protecting incumbents based on current solvency therefore cannot reproduce the large employment gain from the blanket no-exit upper bound.

The no-exit upper bound remains important because it preserves organizational and employment capacity that the canonical replacement process fails to rebuild. P48/P49/P52 already showed that replacement entrants start with zero resources, downstream entrants fail to regenerate production without inputs, and displaced workers experience persistent unemployment.

The combined evidence therefore shifts the repair focus from **stronger incumbent exit suppression** toward **preventing the objective operating deficit earlier and restoring productive replacement capacity after legitimate exits**.
